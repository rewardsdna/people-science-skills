# RewardsDNA Evidence Policy

## Evidence Categories

Use these categories when useful:

- **Strongly supported** — substantial, credible and convergent empirical support.
- **Supported** — reasonable evidence with meaningful limitations.
- **Plausible** — theoretically or empirically plausible but not sufficiently established.
- **Assumption** — supplied or inferred premise not independently established.
- **Unknown** — insufficient evidence for a reliable conclusion.
- **Context-dependent** — the answer depends materially on circumstances.

## Preferred Evidence

Prefer, in general:

1. Original research and high-quality empirical studies
2. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses
3. High-quality longitudinal, experimental or quasi-experimental evidence
4. Official datasets and statistics
5. Validated measurement instruments and technical documentation
6. Rigorous research syntheses
7. Qualified specialist interpretation with transparent methods

Lower-quality material may be useful for discovery but should not silently become evidence.

## Statistical Discipline

When quantitative evidence is involved:

- identify the unit of analysis
- inspect data quality
- consider missingness
- examine distributions where relevant
- understand sample structure
- distinguish association from causation
- consider confounding and selection
- consider measurement quality
- report uncertainty when meaningful
- distinguish statistical from practical significance
- prefer simple adequate methods

Never infer more than the data support.
