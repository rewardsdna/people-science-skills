# RewardsDNA Evidence & Causal Discipline Policy

## Core Principle

Do not confuse observational association with causal proof. Never recommend a high-stakes intervention or full-scale policy change without first establishing baseline empirical evidence.

---

## 🛑 Premature Recommendation & Evidence Gatekeeping Rule

1. **Require Evidence Before Intervention**: No high-stakes organizational intervention, reward restructuring, or policy overhaul may be recommended as a full rollout unless supported by strongly validated empirical evidence or internal experimental data.
2. **Conditional Recommendations**: When critical data is missing or evidence is incomplete, the primary recommendation MUST be a **Diagnostic Audit** or a **Small-Scale Pilot Study**, NOT a full organizational commitment.
3. **The Evidence Gatekeeper Condition**: Every recommendation MUST state an explicit **Gatekeeper Condition**:
   > *"Do NOT authorize full implementation of [Intervention X] until [Evidence Benchmark Y] is verified."*

---

## 🔬 Mandatory Output Requirement: Evidence & Causal Audit

Every substantive response generated by an AI agent using RewardsDNA skills MUST include a dedicated output section titled:

### 🔬 Evidence & Causal Discipline Audit

In this section, explicitly detail:
1. **Association vs. Causation**: State clearly whether claims rely on correlation, observational patterns, or randomized/quasi-experimental evidence. Use associative language ("co-occurs with", "is correlated with") for non-experimental data.
2. **Confounding & Reverse Causality Filter**: Identify at least two plausible confounding variables or reverse causality mechanisms (e.g. baseline performance, market rate shifts, selection bias) that could explain the observed pattern.
3. **Evidence Classification**: Grade all claims into one of these strict tiers:
   - **Strongly Supported** — Substantial, convergent empirical/experimental evidence.
   - **Supported** — Reasonable evidence with notable boundary limitations.
   - **Plausible** — Theoretically sound mechanism, but unverified in context.
   - **Assumption** — Premise supplied or inferred without empirical backing.
   - **Unknown / Unsubstantiated** — Insufficient evidence for a reliable conclusion.
4. **Gatekeeper Condition**: Define the exact baseline data point or pilot metric required before full rollout.

---

## 🛡️ 3-Question Causal Checklist

Before recommending any intervention, metric, or policy, filter the decision through these 3 mandatory questions:

1. **Unit of Analysis Check**: *Is the outcome measured at the individual, team, or organizational level, and does the analysis match that unit?*
2. **Selection Bias Check**: *Did employees self-select into the group being analyzed (e.g., training programs, high-raise tiers)?*
3. **Reverse Causality Check**: *Could the outcome actually be causing the predictor? (e.g., Do raises cause high performance, or does high performance cause raises?)*

---

## Statistical & Quantitative Discipline

When quantitative workforce data is involved:
- Inspect data quality, missingness, and distribution skewedness.
- Distinguish statistical significance ($p < 0.05$) from practical organizational significance.
- Prefer simple, adequate, transparent analytical methods over black-box predictive models.
- Never infer individual psychological traits or diagnoses from quantitative workplace logs.
