# Organizational Behavior Decisioning Skill

**Builds on:** `people-science-core-reasoning`

Use this skill together with the Core Reasoning skill whenever the decision or analysis involves workplace behavior, culture, leadership, motivation, teams, norms, power, change, or organizational systems.

---

## Method

Before recommending an OB intervention or policy:
1. **Clarify the decision**: Distinguish the stated question from the underlying organizational problem.
2. **Identify observable behavior**: Describe behavior in concrete terms rather than abstract labels (e.g., "turnover within 90 days" vs. "poor culture").
3. **Question inherited assumptions**: Separate fundamental behavioral mechanisms from pop HR trends and generational myths.
4. **Evaluate empirical evidence & causal rigor**: Audit claims for correlation vs. causation fallacies.
5. **Consider organizational context**: Analyze company stage, size, leadership norms, and boundary conditions.
6. **Examine trade-offs & side-effects**: Make unintended consequences, gaming risks, and morale impacts explicit.
7. **Formulate a calibrated recommendation**: Propose actionable, transparent, monitorable interventions.

---

## OB Guardrails

Do not default to:
- Generic HR best practices or fashionable HR trends.
- Pop psychology or unsupported personality diagnostic tools.
- Generational stereotypes (e.g. Gen Z myths).
- Vendor marketing material or sales whitepapers as evidence.

---

## 🔬 Mandatory Output Section Requirement

Every response MUST include a dedicated section:

### 🔬 Evidence & Causal Discipline Audit
1. **Association vs. Causation**: Identify whether claims rely on correlation or experimental proof. Use associative language for observational workplace data.
2. **Confounding & Reverse Causality**: Identify at least two plausible confounders or selection biases.
3. **Evidence Classification**: Grade evidence explicitly (Strongly Supported / Plausible / Assumption / Unknown).
