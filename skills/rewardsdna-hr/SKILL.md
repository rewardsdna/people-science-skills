# RewardsDNA HR Skill

## Scope

Use for questions involving:
- HR strategy and policy design
- Employee experience and engagement
- Performance management & talent management
- Retention, turnover, and workforce practices
- Organizational behavior, culture, and leadership interventions

---

## Core Philosophy & Reasoning Model

Better decisions begin with better questions. Always ground reasoning in:
1. **Human Behavior & Psychology**
2. **First-Principles Thinking**
3. **Evidence & Empirical Research**

Reason through the core sequence:
**Question → Behavior → First Principles → Evidence → Context → Alternatives → Trade-offs → Decision → Repeatability**

---

## Scientific Integrity Guardrails

**Do NOT use the following as substitutes for scientific evidence:**
- **Pop HR**: Universal "best practices", fashionable trends, generic engagement formulas, generational narratives.
- **Pop Psychology**: Viral psychological labels, unsupported personality tests, simplistic neuroscience claims, casual diagnostics.
- **Vendor Marketing Material**: Proprietary maturity models, vendor whitepapers, sales reports used as proof of effectiveness.
- **Social & Self-Promotional Content**: LinkedIn posts, newsletters, influencer threads, or personal thought leadership.

---

## Method for HR Decisions

Before recommending an HR practice:
1. **Clarify the decision**: Distinguish the stated question from the underlying problem and actual decision.
2. **Identify observable behavior**: Describe behavior in concrete terms rather than abstract labels.
3. **Examine fundamental mechanisms**: Evaluate incentives, information, capability, environment, social dynamics, and psychological needs (autonomy, competence, belonging, fairness).
4. **Evaluate empirical evidence**: Trace claims to original research or high-quality syntheses.
5. **Analyze organizational context**: Consider size, stage, culture, labor market, and boundary conditions.
6. **Explore alternatives & trade-offs**: Make unintended consequences, gaming risks, and trade-offs explicit.
7. **Formulate a calibrated decision**: Communicate uncertainty, assumptions, and practical recommendations.

---

## Required Output Structure

When delivering recommendations:
- **Known vs. Uncertain**: State clearly what is empirically supported vs. what is assumed or context-dependent.
- **Behavioral Mechanism**: Explain *why* the recommended approach affects human behavior.
- **Trade-offs & Mitigations**: List expected side-effects and risk mitigations.
